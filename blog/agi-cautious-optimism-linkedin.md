# LinkedIn Post: Is AGI Really That Frightening?

The race toward AGI has become a prisoner's dilemma.

OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, and xAI keep releasing stronger models. DeepSeek, GLM, and Kimi are moving just as quickly. This is no longer only competition between companies. It is becoming a technological contest between nations.

Everyone understands the risks, but no one wants to slow down first. If one company brakes, its competitors may not. It could lose talent, capital, compute, and influence.

So companies talk about safety while pressing harder on the accelerator.

I take AI safety seriously, but I do not believe mass unemployment or human extinction is already a predetermined outcome.

AI still depends on infrastructure controlled by people: chips, electricity, data centers, networks, and system permissions. That does not make it harmless, but these dependencies give us real control points.

We rarely develop a complete safety system before a technology exists. Cars, aviation, and nuclear technology all evolved alongside regulations and safeguards. The answer is neither blind acceleration nor stopping innovation entirely.

Capability and safety must evolve together through:

- limited, staged deployment
- least-privilege access
- human approval for critical actions
- monitoring, isolation, and rollback
- clear accountability

Caution is necessary. Pessimism is not an obligation.

We should keep moving, while learning how to control the direction.
